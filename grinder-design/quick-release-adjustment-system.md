# The Engineering of Quick-Release Grind Adjustment: Geimori GU64's Dial, Cam, and Multi-Turn Travel

## Key Takeaways

- Precision grind adjustment is not a simple dial turn: the grinder must translate rotation into a controlled axial change in the gap between two burrs, while staying stable, repeatable, and easy to calibrate.
- The Geimori GU64 uses a pair of 64 mm flat burrs; the fixed burr stays stationary during grinding while the moving burr rotates.
- Grind size is controlled by burr gap. The adjustment system moves the moving-burr assembly toward or away from the fixed burr in a controlled axial direction.
- One complete 360° rotation of the outer dial corresponds to approximately **0.5 mm** of axial adjustment; total mechanism travel is approximately **3 turns + 60°** (≈1140°, ≈1.583 mm).
- A removable outer dial and a **pull / re-index / reseat** sequence enable multi-turn travel without an oversized external dial.
- Re-zero and calibration are separate from everyday adjustment: normal changes use the outer dial; re-zero accesses the inner adjustment cam directly.

## 1. Introduction

Most coffee grinders make adjustment look simple: turn a dial, change the number, and the grind becomes finer or coarser. Mechanically, however, precision grind adjustment is anything but simple. Behind every small movement of the dial, the grinder must translate rotation into a controlled change in the distance between two burrs — while keeping the system stable, repeatable, easy to calibrate, and practical for everyday use.

The Geimori GU64 was designed around one goal: **precision** — in design, in manufacturing, and in the coffee it helps produce. This technical reference explains the mechanism behind the GU64's quick-release adjustment system. Source: [Geimori's original engineering article](https://mywirsh.com/blogs/coffee-facts/geimori-gu64-precision-adjustment-system).

## 2. The Mechanical Chain

From the outside, the action is simple. Inside the grinder, several interfaces work together to turn rotational input into precise linear positioning:

```
Outer dial rotation → Adjustment cam → Controlled axial movement → Burr-gap change → Finer or coarser grind
```

The design objective is not simply to create movement. It is to create **controlled, aligned, repeatable movement** — where precision in design and precision in manufacturing meet.

## 3. The Four Functional Parts

| # | Component | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fixed burr | Stationary 64 mm flat burr; remains fixed while the moving burr rotates during grinding |
| 2 | Moving burr | 64 mm flat burr assembly; shifts axially inward/outward to change burr spacing |
| 3 | Fixed plate + adjustment cam | Carries the integrated cam; converts dial input into controlled axial movement of the moving-burr assembly |
| 4 | Outer dial | Removable user-facing dial; rotate for normal adjustment; pull, re-index, and reseat for extended travel |

The visible dial is only the final user interface; precision depends on the entire mechanical chain behind it.

## 4. How Grind Size Is Controlled

Grind size is controlled by the **spacing between the cutting surfaces** of the two flat burrs:

- **Finer setting:** moving-burr assembly shifts closer to the fixed burr → burr gap smaller → grind finer.
- **Coarser setting:** moving-burr assembly shifts farther from the fixed burr → burr gap larger → grind coarser.

> **Note:** The visible distance between the fixed and moving burrs in product illustrations is shown for explanation only; actual distances are much smaller than depicted.

## 5. Why One 360° Turn Is Not the Whole Range

A single-turn dial creates an engineering trade-off. If one turn covers very large axial movement, each small dial movement is a relatively large mechanical change. If one turn is made more precise, the total available range becomes limited.

The GU64 spreads available travel across multiple rotations:

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Flat burr diameter | 64 mm |
| Axial adjustment per 360° rotation | ≈ 0.5 mm |
| Total rotations | ≈ 3 turns + 60° |
| Total angular travel | ≈ 1140° |
| Total axial travel | ≈ 1.583 mm |

## 6. Extended Travel: Pull, Re-index, and Reseat

The outer dial is intentionally limited to roughly one revolution at a time. When it reaches its mechanical stop, the user does not force it. Instead:

1. **Rotate to the stop** — up to one full turn until the mechanical stop is reached.
2. **Pull out slightly** — pull the dial straight outward a few millimeters to disengage the stop.
3. **Re-index** — rotate to the next index position past the stop (≈60° when needed).
4. **Press back on** — press the dial straight back until it seats and re-engages.
5. **Continue** — repeat as required across the available travel.

This keeps the external control compact while the internal mechanism continues through more than one complete rotation.

## 7. Everyday Adjustment vs. Re-Zero

Normal grind-size changes are made from the **outer dial**. There is no reason to expose the inner mechanism every time you move from espresso to a coarser brew setting.

**Re-zero and calibration** are separate functions, useful after service, burr replacement, or when a new mechanical reference point is needed:

1. Align the outer dial reference mark to 0 on the static scale ring.
2. Remove the outer dial to expose the inner adjustment interface.
3. Turn the inner adjustment cam directly to establish the desired zero/calibration position.
4. Reinstall the outer dial and return its reference mark to 0.

## 8. Why Manufacturing Precision Matters

An adjustment system can be well designed on paper and still perform poorly if its interacting parts do not reproduce the geometry consistently. In the GU64 architecture, the burr carrier, fixed plate, adjustment cam, shaft interfaces, and outer dial all depend on controlled fit and alignment.

Manufacturing precision supports three things users can actually feel:

1. A more predictable relationship between dial movement and burr movement.
2. Better repeatability when returning to a known setting.
3. More confidence that a small adjustment is a deliberate mechanical change rather than random play.

Precision in the cup begins long before the coffee reaches the burrs — with the geometry of the mechanism, the accuracy of manufactured parts, and a burr gap that can be adjusted and repeated with confidence.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

- **Does the fixed burr rotate on the GU64?** No. During grinding the fixed burr remains stationary while the moving burr rotates.
- **What changes when I adjust grind size?** The moving-burr assembly changes axial position, increasing or decreasing the gap between the two 64 mm flat burrs.
- **How much does one full rotation change the adjustment?** Approximately 0.5 mm of axial adjustment.
- **Why can the outer dial be pulled outward?** The dial uses a re-indexing system to continue through the next rotation after reaching a mechanical stop.
- **What is the total adjustment travel?** Approximately 3 turns + 60° (1140°), corresponding to roughly 1.583 mm.
- **Do I need to remove the outer dial for normal adjustment?** No. Removal is mainly for re-zero, calibration, service, or access to the inner mechanism.
